## What the numbers say

- **Traffic is overwhelmingly Pinterest-driven, not search-driven.** Pinterest (all source/medium variants combined) is GA4's largest channel by a wide margin in both the current 90-day window and a year ago; Google organic search is a minor channel (17,661 sessions/90d, ~1.6% of the roughly 1.1M total sessions the top-15 source table sums to). The business's traffic base is a social/pin-cadence dependency, not an SEO-defensible moat — consistent with every prior internal report's conclusion that "search is not the story" when things wobble.
- **Value per session is heavily template-dependent, not just source-dependent.** The best pages in the Mediavine top-15 (`/black-sims-4-cc/` at $23.48 RPM, `/new-sims-4-mods-2026/` at $17.64) run 10–35x the RPM of the worst (`/blog/` at $0.60, WP category archives at $1.6–1.7). The $800–1,500/mo WP-template-injection gap identified on 2026-07-30 has not been closed as of this pull — the same three pages (`/blog/`, both `/category/sims-4/*` archives) are still at the bottom of the current top-15 pull.
- **Session RPM is volatile and currently on a two-month downswing.** $21.92 (Jun) → $16.60 (Jul) → $15.09 (Aug), a -31% swing off the June peak, with January 2026 showing an even sharper single-month RPM crater (-43.6% MoM, from $18.74 to $10.57) on rising sessions. This is a demand/CPM-side pattern (ad-market compression), not a traffic problem, matching the diagnosis pattern in every prior Mark/Max report reviewed.
- **Desktop carries the business.** 93.8% of sessions, 96.2% of Mediavine revenue, and a session RPM nearly double mobile's ($15.84 vs $8.81). Any mobile-traffic-growth push would need a mobile-monetization fix first or it dilutes blended RPM.
- **The Google-search channel has collapsed ~94% over 16 months** (38K clicks/mo in May 2025 → ~2–3K/mo for 7 straight months through Aug 2026), a decline an order of magnitude larger than anything the team's recent week-scoped "traffic drop" investigations examined. **This has not been diagnosed anywhere in the report history reviewed** — every prior Search/GSC report scoped its analysis to a 1–4 week window and concluded "not a search story," which is true for those short windows but leaves this much larger structural decline completely unexamined.
- **Concentration risk is high on two fronts at once**: channel concentration (Pinterest dominance, with Direct's -30.5% WoW move on 08-20 still unexplained) and page concentration (a handful of URLs — homepage, `/new-sims-4-mods-2026/`, `/black-sims-4-cc/`, `/must-have-mods-sims-4/` — account for a disproportionate share of both sessions and revenue in every pull reviewed).
- **Two unresolved data-quality anomalies sit underneath all of the above and haven't been reconciled by any report on file:** (1) GA4 shows Bing organic at 223,258 sessions/90d — 12.6x Google organic — which Search Console's Google-only click data does not corroborate at any plausible ratio; (2) a `(not set)` landing page absorbed 15,882 sessions in the last 30 days at a 3.4% engagement rate (vs. 65–84% site-wide), suggesting bot/tag-misfire contamination sitting inside the headline session totals used throughout this fact base and every report that cites them.
- **GA4 has zero conversion instrumentation.** Every source/medium row returns `conversions: 0`, and the 30-day event list contains no signup, lead, or subscribe event. Whatever the newsletter form and premium-intent banner are doing today, none of it reaches GA4 as a measurable event.
- **Patreon revenue, newsletter subscriber count, and premium-intent signup volume are not measured anywhere in this codebase.** Patreon appears only as a scraping source for other creators' content. The only newsletter/subscriber numbers on file are 2026-strategy-doc aspirational targets, never actuals. The premium-intent banner (this session's own branch topic) has no GA4 event and no report has ever cited a real count for it.
- **GSC's own sitemap-summary endpoint claims 0 of 16,550 submitted URLs are indexed**, which contradicts both live click data (dozens of distinct URLs generating clicks in the last 90 days, per §3) and prior spot-checks that passed via `index_inspect` — an unreconciled metric that should be treated as "unknown," not "healthy" or "broken."
